Looking through old folders recently, I came across a trail of drawings spanning more than a decade.

Back in school, I drew constantly. Then college started, physics took over, and I didn't touch a pencil for nearly ten years. The itch only returned years later during graduate school—mostly in the margins of lecture notes, on scrap paper, or whenever an equation stubbornly refused to solve itself.

Here are a few surviving artifacts from 2006 to 2018:

---

### 1. Landscape with Cart (2006)

![Landscape with Cart](/assets/img/posts/sketches/2006-landscape.jpg){: .sketch-img }
_Graphite &middot; 2006_

From a local school art competition that I somehow won. I spent days worrying about getting the perspective right on that wagon so the wheels wouldn't look like squashed eggs. It ended up framed on a wall at home—proof that back then, I had way more patience than I do now.

---

### 2. The Chef (2008)

![The Chef](/assets/img/posts/sketches/2008-chef.jpg){: .sketch-img }
_Graphite &middot; 2008_

One of the last sketches from that period. If the background looks like hours of cross-hatching, it wasn't—I just shaded the paper over the rough back of an exam board.

After this, the sketchbooks went into storage for a decade.

---

### 3. Kafka on the Couch (2018)

![I Dreamed I Was Kafka](/assets/img/posts/sketches/2018-kafka.jpg){: .sketch-img }
_Ink &middot; September 2018_

When drawing came back, I picked up a set of Sakura Microns and copied this cartoon I came across. Gregor Samsa lying on a psychoanalyst's couch complaining about his dreams felt uncomfortably relatable.

---

### 4. Pencil Study (2018)

![Pencil Study](/assets/img/posts/sketches/2018-portrait-study.jpg){: .sketch-img }
_Graphite &middot; May 2018_

In 2018, I tried getting back into pencil portraits, gave up halfway, and covered the right side with paper to pretend it was an artistic composition choice.
